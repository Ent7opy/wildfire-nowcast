## Professional voice patterns

Fire-Twitter during named events clusters around a very small number of trusted operator voices, not around tools. Watch Duty (nonprofit, @watchdutyapp) is the single most quoted, quoted-back, and amplified source during a live fire in the US. Official agencies (CalFire, NWS offices, NOAA Satellites, NSW RFS, Copernicus EMS) broadcast; they don't argue. Operator-peer argument happens almost exclusively in Watch Duty's replies and quote-tweets.

Consensus among pros: (a) official perimeter and incident data lags real-time by hours, which is unacceptable during wind-driven events; (b) radio-scanner + human-verification is the current ground truth; (c) software that purports to replace that loop gets treated as marketing, not as tool. Dissensus: whether citizen-facing apps (Watch Duty) are "official" enough to be cited by agencies — CalFire's on-record position per CBS News is that they "should not be regarded as official sources," a view Watch Duty itself contested inline during LA 2025.

## Named-event case studies

**LA Palisades / Eaton, Jan 7–14 2025.** Watch Duty added 600K users in 24h, hit 100K requests/sec, kept <20ms response with Fastly. The discourse moment was less about fire than about which tool was legitimate: Watch Duty's operator voice (John Mills) openly rejected the "we'll get acquired" narrative ("I just don't give a shit") and its account publicly sparred with Musk on-platform. CalFire's own-channel output was visible but minor in citation count compared with WD.

**Canada / NWT wildfires 2023.** The failure mode was not tool-lag — it was AI-generated fake imagery. NWT Fire called circulating AI photos of flames near Fort Providence "sensationalized slop." BC Wildfire Service issued parallel alerts. First visible case of agencies having to counter-program generative AI in live wildfire comms.

**Greece Rhodes 2023.** Copernicus EMS delivered 47 maps across 8 AoIs in 12 days and broadcast this as success. No critical operator voices surfaced via my search path. Coverage gap; non-English fire-Twitter is under-sampled.

## AI-in-wildfire takes

Serious practitioner consensus this pass: AI tools are not yet trusted for live decisions. Positions sampled:

- Watch Duty's core brand ("the magic isn't in the software, it's in the people") is an explicit counter-thesis to AI-forward wildfire products.
- Academic review of WildfireGPT (PMC): "lacks the specialized training, real-time data integration, and algorithmic precision required for reliable wildfire forecasting."
- Live-event failure mode is now **AI disinformation**, not AI tooling — the pain users express is about being misled by fake imagery, forcing agencies to act as debunkers (NWT Fire, BC Wildfire Service). This is a problem a well-built tool could actually help solve: provenance + agreement-with-official-layer indicators for circulating imagery.

Skeptic-leaning voices dominate; no serious practitioner tweets surfaced praising an LLM-based fire product by name. Technosylva + WIFIRE (model-based, not LLM-based) get measured, enterprise-tier praise in press but almost no citizen-Twitter presence.

## Surprises (PM attention)

1. **Watch Duty moat is ideological, not just technical.** "The magic isn't in the software, it's in the people" is a direct wall against any AI-first wildfire consumer product in US/CA — WFN competing in that lane walks into an armed nonprofit with first-responder credibility.
2. **AI disinformation is emerging as a distinct live-fire pain point** (NWT 2023, Canadian 2025 season). Agencies currently act as manual debunkers. Technical problem a tool could address: provenance, official-layer agreement, automatic flagging of circulating imagery that does not match FIRMS / agency perimeters.
3. **Professional voices have quietly migrated** (Bluesky, Mastodon, LinkedIn) post-2024 X changes. Track A was not sampling IMET personal accounts; they may not be on X anymore. Worth a follow-up pass if fire-professional voice is a priority.
