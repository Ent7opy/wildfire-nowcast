# Outreach plan v1 — second-archetype contacts

**Date:** 2026-05-07
**Author:** PM_CLAUDE
**Trigger:** product review 2026-05-07 §4 ("Distribution") and §5 #11.
**Status:** Plan B for week 2 of post-launch if the LTA WRN newsletter slot
(launch-acceptance #9) does not convert.

## Why this exists

The A' v1 launch commits to a single distribution channel: a guest post in
the Land Trust Alliance Western Regional Network (LTA WRN) newsletter. The
product review correctly noted that no second channel is staged — eight
archetypes were named in the thesis (`pm/north-star.md`), v1 reaches one,
and a 4–8 week silent gap is the realistic downside if the LTA slot doesn't
land or doesn't convert.

This plan identifies one outreach contact each in three named archetype-2/3
networks: **IPBN** (Iberian conservation), **EUROPARC** (European protected-
area network), and **IJNR** (US natural-resource journalism). The goal is
*identification only*: a path to an email or a public profile we can reach,
plus the one-sentence pitch we'd lead with. **Do not email until v1 has
shipped** — premature outreach with a half-finished tool is worse than no
outreach.

## Constraints

- **No fabricated names.** This document names organisations, public-facing
  roles, and public contact channels only. If a specific person is named,
  they must be cited from a public website, public profile, or public
  publication. If we can't confirm a name from a public source, the
  contact path is the role + general intake.
- **No commercial framing.** Earth Tools is non-profit (per `pm/PM_CLAUDE.md`
  Constraints); the pitch leads with stewardship + free + open, not
  "tooling vendor."
- **One sentence per pitch.** Anything longer signals desperation.

## Contact 1 — IPBN (Iberian / Portuguese stewardship)

**Organisation candidate:** **WWF Portugal** (Associação Natureza Portugal —
ANP/WWF), Lisbon. WWF's Portuguese affiliate runs the "Portugal Forests"
programme focused on Mediterranean forest restoration and fire
resilience, which puts it in the exact A' archetype-3 slice
(Mediterranean Natura 2000 / conservation steward) the thesis names.

**Why this org over a generic "IPBN" target:** the original product-review
recommendation said "IPBN" — the Iberian / Portuguese stewardship community
— without naming a specific organisation. There is no single legal entity
called "IPBN" with a public web presence I can confirm; the closest
mappings are WWF Portugal (ANP), Quercus (oldest Portuguese conservation
NGO), and LPN (Liga para a Protecção da Natureza). WWF Portugal is the
strongest first contact because (a) it operates inside the Mediterranean
Natura 2000 network the thesis explicitly targets, (b) it has an explicit
forest-fire programme (per the public website), and (c) WWF International's
network gives a single positive response disproportionate downstream reach.

**Contact path:** general organisational intake.
- Website: https://www.natureza-portugal.org/ (ANP/WWF Portugal, public).
- Form: site contact form, or general info email surfaced on the site.
- LinkedIn: ANP/WWF Portugal organisation page.

**Named individual:** none cited here. The "Forest programme manager" or
"Florestas / Forestry programme lead" role is the right inbound; a name
should be confirmed from the org's published team page at the moment of
outreach (avoid out-of-date info baked into this doc).

**One-sentence pitch:**
> "We've built a free, open tool that watches the specific polygons your
> stewards care about and sends a plain-language brief when something
> changes — happy to give your forest team a guided look in 15 minutes."

**Source citation:** WWF Portugal (Associação Natureza Portugal) public
site, https://www.natureza-portugal.org/ — the org's public web presence
naming its forest-restoration and conservation programme. *(Dev-agent /
PM should re-verify the current URL before outreach; orgs change domains.)*

**Fallback if WWF Portugal doesn't respond:** Quercus
(https://www.quercus.pt/) and LPN (https://www.lpn.pt/) are the two
next-priority Portuguese conservation NGOs; both have public web presences
and operate in the same archetype.

## Contact 2 — EUROPARC Federation (European protected-area network)

**Organisation:** **EUROPARC Federation** — the umbrella body for European
protected areas (national parks, regional parks, nature reserves). Based
in Regensburg, Germany. Member organisations include site managers across
the EU and adjacent countries — exactly the archetype-3 "Natura 2000 site
manager" the thesis names.

**Why this contact:** EUROPARC is the highest-leverage single inbound for
European protected-area site managers. One LinkedIn post or one newsletter
mention from EUROPARC reaches the same network it would take 100
individual-park emails to reach. The federation also has a Communication
team whose job description includes surfacing tools that help member
sites — this is a structurally hospitable inbound.

**Contact path:** public organisational channels.
- Website: https://www.europarc.org/ (federation HQ, public).
- General info email surfaced on the site's contact page.
- LinkedIn: EUROPARC Federation organisation page (public).
- The federation publishes an annual "EUROPARC Conference" and a regular
  newsletter — the conference call-for-contributions is a second
  contact path if direct intake doesn't respond.

**Named individual:** none cited here. The "Communications Manager" or
"Communications & Events Officer" role is the right inbound for a tool-
introduction pitch; the "Policy Officer — Climate / Natural Disasters"
role is the right inbound for a policy-relevance framing. Confirm names
from the EUROPARC team page at the moment of outreach.

**One-sentence pitch:**
> "We've built a free, AI-assisted tool that watches a protected area's
> polygons for fire activity and sends a plain-language situation brief —
> we'd love to add it to the next EUROPARC tools-for-members round-up."

**Source citation:** EUROPARC Federation public site,
https://www.europarc.org/ — the federation's public web presence and
member directory. *(Re-verify the current URL and team page before
outreach.)*

**Fallback:** if the federation HQ doesn't respond, individual member
organisations like Parques de Sintra (PT) or Parc Naturel Régional du
Verdon (FR) are the next layer down — both publicly named, both in
fire-prone Mediterranean / sub-Mediterranean regions.

## Contact 3 — IJNR (Institutes for Journalism and Natural Resources)

**Organisation:** **Institutes for Journalism and Natural Resources (IJNR)**
— US-based nonprofit running fellowships and field expeditions for
journalists covering natural-resource issues, including wildfire,
conservation, and Indigenous land stewardship. Operates throughout the
US West, with annual programmes that bring journalists into fire-affected
landscapes.

**Why this contact:** IJNR is the highest-leverage single inbound for the
"environmental journalist covering named fire seasons" archetype the
thesis names. Their journalist alumni network has documented coverage of
fire seasons across the US West. One IJNR newsletter mention or one
"useful tool" Slack note from a programme director reaches more
fire-beat journalists than a year of cold pitching individual reporters.

**Contact path:** public organisational channels.
- Website: https://ijnr.org/ (IJNR, public).
- Site contact form or general info email surfaced on the contact page.
- LinkedIn: IJNR organisation page (public).
- The org publishes an "Apply" page for journalist fellowships — do NOT
  contact via the application path; that's for applicants. Use the
  general / press intake instead.

**Named individual:** none cited here. The "Executive Director" or
"Programs Director" role is the right inbound for a tool-introduction
pitch. The IJNR site lists current staff with names and roles publicly;
confirm at the moment of outreach.

**One-sentence pitch:**
> "We've built a free, open tool that lets a journalist 'watch' a specific
> place — a community, a watershed, a tribal territory — and get a
> plain-language brief whenever fire activity changes near it; happy to
> add it to your next field-tools list."

**Source citation:** IJNR public site, https://ijnr.org/ — the org's
public web presence describing its fellowships and natural-resource
journalism mission. *(Re-verify the current URL and team page before
outreach.)*

**Fallback:** if IJNR doesn't respond, **SEJ (Society of Environmental
Journalists, https://www.sej.org/)** is the larger US environmental-
journalism membership org and a parallel path; their tip-sheet feature
("EJToday") is a documented inbound for tools.

## Sequencing

**Do not contact any of the three before A' v1 has shipped and the LTA WRN
post is live.** Premature outreach burns the inbound on a half-finished
artifact.

**Trigger conditions for contacting:**
- 14 days post-launch with <5 sign-ups attributable to the LTA WRN post →
  contact WWF Portugal first (smallest org, fastest decision cycle).
- 21 days post-launch → contact EUROPARC.
- 28 days post-launch → contact IJNR.

Sequence is intentional: the smallest org first lets us iterate on the
pitch with low downside; EUROPARC and IJNR see a more refined pitch by the
time we get to them.

## Things this plan deliberately does NOT do

- Does **not** name specific individuals when the public sources I can cite
  don't carry the role-to-name mapping with confidence. The product
  review's recommendation #11 said "have the contact identified"; the
  honest interpretation is "know which org and which role to ask for,"
  not "have a name in this doc that may be wrong by the time we reach
  out."
- Does **not** include archetype-1 (LTA / land trusts) — that channel is
  the v1 launch path, not the second-archetype fallback.
- Does **not** include archetype-4 (Indigenous fire crews / Indigenous
  Peoples Burning Network), Firewise communities, or LTER scientists.
  Those each deserve their own outreach plan; this v1 stays focused on the
  three the product review explicitly named.
- Does **not** draft full email templates. The one-sentence pitch is the
  load-bearing piece; the rest of the email is small-talk and a link, and
  should be written at the moment of outreach with whatever specifics
  about the recipient are then publicly available.
