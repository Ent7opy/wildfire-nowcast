# Brief 10 — Non-profit user archetype scout

## Why this exists

Candidate A was reframed from "B2B operator" to "anyone with a place to protect" under a non-profit model. That's a statement of scope, not a validated user. This brief produces the real, named, public-footprint list of user archetypes who would plausibly adopt the tool.

**Read first:**
1. `pm/PM_CLAUDE.md`
2. `pm/decisions/0003-nonprofit-and-free-infra-constraints.md`
3. `pm/backlog.md` § "A"
4. `pm/research-log/2026-04-21-reddit.md` + `2026-04-21-non-na-geography.md` + `2026-04-21-github.md` — for pre-existing user signal

## Goal

For each non-profit-compatible archetype, produce 3–5 named real-world examples with public signals of the pain. No personas, no hypotheticals.

## Target archetypes

1. **Conservation NGOs with land holdings** — land trusts, Nature Conservancy state chapters, national trusts outside US
2. **Indigenous fire stewards / First Nations fire crews** — cultural-burning practitioners, First Nations wildfire services, Aboriginal ranger programs (AU, CA, BR)
3. **Protected-area managers** — national park authorities (small/mid-country), biosphere reserves, Natura 2000 site managers
4. **Small municipalities in fire country** — Greek Δήμοι, Portuguese freguesias, California WUI cities, Australian regional councils
5. **WUI homeowners with skin in the game** — organized HOAs in fire-prone areas, defensible-space community groups
6. **Diaspora / expats** — members of fire-country diaspora communities monitoring family property remotely
7. **Researchers with ongoing field sites** — long-term ecological research (LTER) sites, phenology networks, individual field stations
8. **Journalists covering named fire seasons** — regional newsroom fire beats, freelance environmental reporters

## Method

For each archetype, use WebSearch / WebFetch / public social + org directories to:
- Find 3–5 named real-world examples (organization name + URL)
- Capture one public signal of fire-related pain per example (blog post, report, tweet, job listing, funded project)
- Identify the plausible distribution channel (how this person/org finds out the tool exists)
- Estimate size of the cohort (rough order of magnitude, globally)

## Output

**`pm/research-log/2026-04-21-user-archetypes.md`** — ≤1000 words:
- `## Archetype cards` — one per archetype (8 total): named examples with URLs, pain signal, distribution channel, rough cohort size, fit rating (1–5) for candidate A
- `## Strongest archetypes` — top 3 ranked, with reasoning
- `## Long-tail archetypes` — 2–3 that didn't make the top 3 but shouldn't be dismissed
- `## Archetypes to explicitly deprioritize` — any that looked promising on paper but evidence thins out
- `## Distribution implications` — what does "launch" look like for a non-profit tool reaching these users? (blog posts in specific communities, Reddit threads, conservation newsletters, academic networks, HN post — concrete channels)

**`pm/signals/2026-04-21-user-archetypes-raw.md`** — all URLs used, grouped by archetype.

## Constraints

- No invented user quotes. Every claim links to a URL.
- "Could exist" is not evidence. "Here's a named example with a URL" is.
- If an archetype turns out to be weaker than expected, say so.

~35 min.
