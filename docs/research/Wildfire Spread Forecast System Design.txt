Production Design for Spatio-Temporal Wildfire Spread Forecasting
The escalation of catastrophic wildfire events necessitates a transition from empirical, physics-based models to high-fidelity, data-driven systems capable of integrating multimodal environmental telemetry at scale. The current iteration of the wildfire-nowcast repository, centered on a pixel-wise gradient-boosted baseline, provides a foundational entry point for spread prediction but lacks the spatial awareness and uncertainty quantification required for high-stakes operational decision-making.1 The path toward a production-ready 24/48/72-hour forecast system involves a systematic overhaul of the modeling architecture, data ingestion pipelines, and verification protocols, ensuring that predictions are not only accurate but also statistically calibrated and operationally resilient.4
Section A: Executive Decision Summary
The following strategic directives define the next evolution of the wildfire forecasting system, prioritizing spatial intelligence, probabilistic rigor, and automated governance.
* The system shall transition from the current HistGradientBoostingClassifier to a deep convolutional U-Net architecture to capture multi-scale spatial dependencies and fire-front topology.7
* The modeling framework will adopt a "Balanced" implementation, utilizing a 2D U-Net with temporal channel-stacking to optimize the tradeoff between predictive lift and inference latency.7
* Fuel moisture dynamics, encompassing both Live Fuel Moisture Content (LFMC) and Dead Fuel Moisture Content (DFMC), are identified as the primary feature gap and will be integrated into all future training cycles.11
* Near-Real-Time (NRT) satellite telemetry from the VIIRS 375m sensor will be prioritized over MODIS 1km data due to its superior spatial consistency and lower temporal jitter.14
* A "Global-to-Regional" data strategy will be implemented, utilizing pre-training on global datasets followed by localized fine-tuning for specific fire regimes like the Mediterranean or the Western United States.17
* Explicit uncertainty quantification will be achieved via a post-processing calibration layer using Isotonic Regression and Platt Scaling to ensure forecast probabilities align with observed frequencies.4
* The primary evaluation metric for model promotion will be the Brier Skill Score (BSS), which accounts for the inherent uncertainty in the climatological base rate.4
* Spatial verification will be modernized using the Structure-Amplitude-Location (SAL) metric to evaluate fire-front shape and displacement, mitigating the limitations of pixel-wise accuracy.23
* A strict MLOps promotion gate will be enforced, requiring the "Challenger" model to demonstrate a statistically significant improvement over the "Champion" model via the Diebold-Mariano test.26
* The system will maintain a "Fail-Open" reliability policy, where missing weather or fuel moisture inputs trigger a fallback to persistence-based heuristic forecasts.29
* Shadow deployment will be the mandatory precursor to any production rollout, allowing the challenger to receive live traffic without influencing the primary forecast.5
* An Area of Interest (AOI) cap of 40,000 cells per forecast will be strictly maintained to ensure inference latency remains within the 2-minute operational Service Level Objective (SLO).6
* Monitoring systems will alert on "Concept Drift" when the relationship between meteorological drivers (e.g., wind speed) and spread rates deviates from the training manifold.5
* The training pipeline will implement a strict temporal/spatial split, ensuring that data from individual fire events is never shared across training and validation sets to prevent leakage.5
* The hindcast_dataset.py logic will be updated to include Fire Radiative Power (FRP) as a proxy for burn intensity, enhancing the signal for fire-front propagation.14

Section C: Production Specification
1. Data Strategy and Integrated Environmental Drivers
The shift from a heuristic-based system to a deep-learning-based spread forecaster requires a massive expansion of the feature set beyond the current simple weather and slope inputs.11 Wildfire behavior is a complex interaction of fuels, topography, and atmospheric conditions, many of which are dynamic on sub-daily timescales.12
Required Feature Groups
The existing feature set in configs/spread_train_v1.yaml (slope, aspect, wind) must be expanded to include the following groups:
* Fuel Moisture and Loading: Static fuel maps (e.g., World Cover Map) must be augmented with dynamic moisture signals.18 Live Fuel Moisture Content (LFMC) is a critical indicator of the flammability of the vegetation canopy, while Dead Fuel Moisture Content (DFMC) dictates the receptivity of surface fuels to ignition.12
* Atmospheric Stability and Moisture: Relative humidity, 2-meter temperature, and 24-hour accumulated precipitation are essential for modeling the drying or wetting of fuels.32 Additionally, the inclusion of indices like the Burning Index (BI) or Energy Release Component (ERC) from the National Fire Danger Rating System (NFDRS) provides the model with pre-computed physical proxies for fire intensity.1
* Topographic Correction: Beyond raw slope and aspect, the model should ingest a "ruggedness" index or Topographic Position Index (TPI) to identify valley bottoms and ridge lines, which act as natural accelerants or barriers to spread.18
Sensor Handling and Data Quality
The production system must navigate the tradeoff between Near-Real-Time (NRT) latency and Science-Quality precision.14

The hindcast_dataset.py uses a   3-hour window for fire labels, which is an acceptable compromise given the revisit frequency of polar-orbiting satellites.14 However, to prevent data leakage, the temporal split must be strictly enforced: models should be trained on fires from previous years and validated on fires from the most recent season.5 Spatial splitting (e.g., training on California, testing on Oregon) is also required to ensure regional generalization.17
2. Modeling Architecture: The Spatial U-Net
The core modeling change involves moving from the pixel-wise HistGradientBoostingClassifier in ml/train_spread_v1.py to a 2D U-Net. The current model ignores the spatial context of neighboring cells, whereas a U-Net extracts hierarchical features that represent the fire's structure and its surrounding environment.7
Architectural Components
* Encoder Path: Successive convolutional layers and max-pooling operations reduce spatial dimensions while increasing feature depth, capturing large-scale drivers like synoptic wind patterns or major topographic features.7
* Decoder Path: Upsampling and transposed convolutions restore the spatial resolution, guided by skip connections that pass fine-grained details (like a narrow fuel break) directly from the encoder.7
* Temporal Stacking: Instead of a single input image, the model will ingest a    tensor, where    represents the preceding time steps. This allows the model to "learn" the velocity and direction of the fire front as a vector.7

3. Explicit Uncertainty and Calibration
In a wildfire context, a raw model score is not decision-usable. An incident commander needs to know the probability of a ridge being breached. The output of the U-Net must be passed through the ml/calibration.py module to produce a well-calibrated probabilistic forecast.4
Calibration Techniques
   * Isotonic Regression: This non-parametric approach is ideal when the sample size is large, as it can correct complex non-linear biases in the model's confidence.4
   * Platt Scaling: If training data for a specific region is sparse, Platt Scaling (logistic regression on model outputs) provides a more stable, albeit less flexible, calibration.4
Verification Diagnostics
The system shall generate Reliability Diagrams for every model run. These diagrams plot the predicted probability against the observed frequency of fire.20 A well-calibrated system will track the 45-degree diagonal. Deviations to the right (over-forecasting) or left (under-forecasting) will serve as triggers for model recalibration or rollback.5

	5. Reliability and Operations
Operational wildfire forecasting is a mission-critical service. The system must be designed for "Fail-Open" reliability, ensuring that some forecast—even if simplified—is always available.5
Fallback and Fail-Open Policy
      * Weather Outage: If the HRRR/NWP feed fails, the service.py module will trigger a fallback to persistence-based forecasting, assuming zero wind and constant humidity.29
      * Staleness Thresholds: Forecasts using satellite data older than 12 hours will be automatically flagged as "Low Confidence" and will not be promoted to the public dashboard.16
Service Level Objectives (SLOs)
      * Freshness: The system must ingest new satellite hotspots and issue an updated forecast within 15 minutes of the telemetry arriving at the FIRMS/LANCE ingest.6
      * Availability: 99.9% uptime for the inference API during the wildfire season.5
      * Quality: 95% of 24h forecasts must fall within the 10% calibration margin on the Reliability Diagram.20
Monitoring and Alerting
The system will monitor for "Silent Failure" where the model continues to output predictions but their skill drops significantly.
      * Data Drift: Alert if the distribution of input features (e.g., wind speed) deviates from the training distribution by more than 2 standard deviations.5
      * Calibration Drift: Automated weekly audits of the Reliability Score; any deviation exceeding 0.15 triggers an immediate alert and a potential rollback to the previous champion.5

	Section F: Open Questions for Product and Operations
The following questions require consensus before the system moves to Phase 3 of development.
                  1. Risk Bias Preference: Does the operational team prefer a "Safety First" bias (higher recall, over-predicting the fire area) or an "Efficiency First" bias (higher precision, reducing false alarms)?.5
                  2. Human-in-the-Loop Thresholds: At what staleness level should the automated forecast be entirely blocked? If VIIRS data is 18 hours old, is a stale forecast better than no forecast?.16
                  3. Regional vs. Global Model: Should we deploy a single "Global" model with regional features, or maintain 10-15 "Regional" models (e.g., California, Mediterranean, boreal) with separate weights?.17
                  4. UI/UX Probabilities: How should the explicit uncertainty be visualized? Options include a "Most Likely Extent" line vs. a "Heatmap of Probability" vs. "Conformal Prediction Sets" representing a 90% confidence region.21
                  5. Compute Budget: Can the inference infrastructure support GPUs, or must the U-Net be optimized for CPU-only execution to meet current cost constraints?.10
The proposed transition to a spatially-aware, calibrated, and gated forecasting system ensures that the wildfire-nowcast repository evolves from an experimental tool into a mission-critical operational asset. By centering the design on spatial U-Nets and probabilistic verification, the system provides incident commanders with the clarity and statistical rigor required to protect lives and property in an increasingly volatile fire landscape.2
